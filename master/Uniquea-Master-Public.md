**Covariance, covariant and invariant: three meanings (Established; filed November 4, 2026).**

- *Covariance (statistics):* how two quantities vary together; for example, the Pantheon+
  covariance matrix. It measures only the linear part of a relationship. Non-linear dependence
  needs other tools, such as mutual information or distance correlation (Székely, 2007).
- *Invariants and covariants (algebra):* under a linear change of variables, an invariant is
  unchanged (for example, the discriminant b² − 4ac), and a covariant changes in a fixed,
  predictable way (Cayley and Sylvester, 1800s).
- *Invariant and covariant (physics):* an invariant is a value every observer agrees on (rest
  mass, c, the spacetime interval). A covariant law keeps the same form for every observer.
  Special relativity is covariant under linear (Lorentz) changes. General relativity demands
  covariance under any smooth, non-linear change of coordinates, and its field equations are
  themselves non-linear.
- *One Math (Candidate):* the dimensionless ladder (F/F*)^(2ⁿ) = (m/m*)(a/a*)^(2ⁿ⁺¹) keeps its
  form under a change of units, a modest scale covariance. *Open:* general covariance, meaning a
  form that holds in every coordinate system.
