## What happened at 380,000 years

*Established.*

- Before then the universe was a glowing fog of charged gas; light could not travel far before being scattered.
- At about 380,000 years it cooled to about 3,000 K. Electrons settled onto nuclei, atoms formed (**recombination**), and **light broke free for the first time**.
- That freed light, stretched by the universe's expansion into microwaves, is the **cosmic microwave background**: the oldest picture we have.
- It has been called the **smoking gun** of a hot, dense beginning since Arno Penzias and Robert Wilson found it in 1965, after first blaming the hiss in their antenna on pigeons.

## How dense was it?

*Established, with one correction.*

| Then (380,000 years) | Today |
|---|---|
| about 300 atoms per cubic centimeter | about 1 atom per 4 cubic meters, on average |
| about 3,000 K | 2.7 K (the same light, cooled by expansion) |
| smooth to about 1 part in 100,000 | galaxies, stars, planets, people |

- *Correction:* the universe at 380,000 years was not a region "so dense." It was about a billion times denser than space today, yet still emptier than the best vacuum made in a laboratory, and astonishingly smooth.

## What holds: source material kept close

*Established.*

- The slightly denser patches, a few parts in 100,000, **did not dissipate**. Gravity kept their material close and drew in more.
- Over hundreds of millions of years those patches grew into the first stars and galaxies.
- So the faint ripples in the background light are the **seeds** of everything: the source material kept near the infant universe, to grow from.

- **The birth canal:** the passage from opaque fog to clear space is a birth: the first light out, like a first cry.
- **The navel:** the ripples are where the newborn universe was still joined to what fed it.
